# P1.2 replay causal ablation

## Question

Does the replay buffer itself explain any advantage over a matched online learner, or is the effect simply caused by
updating the backbone on the stream?

## Arms

The fixed replay confirmation already supplies two arms on the same protocol:

1. an exact frozen warm-started twin, and
1. full-backbone online MAE adaptation with compute-matched reservoir replay.

This run adds the third arm: full-backbone online MAE adaptation with `AcceptAll` (no replay). The no-replay arm uses
the same warm checkpoint, five training seeds, stream partition, learning rate (`1e-4`), update cadence, batch budget,
evaluation panels, and probe recipes as the replay arm.

## Primary comparison

The causal effect is `replay adapted - no-replay adapted`, averaged over the three predeclared probe panels within each
training seed. Intervals are paired Student-t intervals over the five training seeds. The frozen arm remains a reference
for both online arms, not an additional source of independent replication.

The same decision rule is used for the broad replay claim: support requires positive lower 95% intervals for both global
kNN and mean final-condition accuracy, at least four positive seeds, and at least +2 pp mean kNN improvement. A
non-positive upper interval on either primary metric contradicts the claim; otherwise the result is inconclusive.

## Scope and limitations

- One shared warm checkpoint and one attribute-defined BDD regime order.
- Three retained scene classes, not detection or all BDD tasks.
- Validation panels are development-validation data, not the untouched test split.
- Probe panels are averaged within seed and are not treated as independent replications.
