## Questions and interpretation fixed before execution

1. Does last-block adaptation improve accuracy over the exact frozen initialization?
1. Does it reduce forgetting relative to full-backbone no-replay learning without losing accuracy?

For each accuracy measure, average panels within seed, then compute five paired differences and 95% Student-t intervals
(df=4). Report last-block minus frozen, last-block minus full no-replay, and full no-replay minus frozen for kNN, global
linear probe, and mean final-condition accuracy. Report each panel separately. Conditions receive equal weight,
including sparse conditions.

For consistency with the preceding study, apply its joint frozen-advantage rule: both kNN and final-condition intervals
above zero, at least four positive seeds each, and mean kNN gain >=2 pp. A broader accuracy claim also requires the
global linear interval above zero.

A retention/accuracy development candidate requires lower forgetting than full no-replay (upper 95% interval for
last-block minus full forgetting below zero), and no material accuracy loss against full no-replay (lower intervals
above -1 pp for all three accuracy measures). The -1 pp margin is a declared development screen, not a project-wide
standard. Report BWT as well. Failure to pass this screen leaves the candidate unestablished; it does not prove
equivalence.

This is an exploratory development comparison on reused seeds and validation panels. Intervals describe training-seed
variation conditional on a shared checkpoint, dataset, regime order, and panels. A positive candidate needs fresh
training seeds and broader evaluation. No conclusion about all BDD tasks, other checkpoints, larger models, or all
less-plastic methods follows. Retention trajectories are measured on panel 0; sparse conditions can dominate their
averages.
