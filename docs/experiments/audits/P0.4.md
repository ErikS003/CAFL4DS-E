# Audit — P0.4.x instability / divergence-mode instrument calibration (2026-08-12)

Peer-review audit of the instability failure-mode sub-studies (P0.4.0 LR-driven divergence → P0.4.1 data
surprise/shock), read against the substudy docs, their `latest-results` distillates, and the promoted artifacts under
[`../phase0/artifacts/`](../phase0/artifacts/) (`P0.4.0/{dose_seed0,dose_seed1,batch16_seed0}`,
`P0.4.1/{gate_seed0,gate_seed1,dataset_scrambled,scale_dose,scale_dose_seeded}`). It reviews *conclusions*, not
arithmetic — the numbers were spot-checked against the JSON gates and reproduce (the two-seed divergence dose-response,
the batch-16 ablation, the two-seed surprise gate, the phase-scramble non-fire, and the amplitude ladder were all
re-read from the artifacts).

Every point below has the same shape: **Issue** (what the audit found), **Remediation** (what to do — tagged `[wording]`
doc-edit / `[analysis]` post-hoc / `[engineering]` build-and-confirm / `[new runs]` re-run an existing config /
`[new substudy]` / `[decision]` / `[defer → Phase-1]`), and **Conclusion** (the outcome after remediation). The
remediation pass is **complete**: every Conclusion is filled (led by a bold verdict label), and the synthesis lands in
[§F](#f-disposition--the--after-remediation) — the mode proceeds to Phase 1. The code-validity check (E) was stated
as-found up front, since it gates whether the rest of the audit can be trusted.

## Verdict

P0.4 is the cleanest of Phase 0's modes, and honestly so — it is the one mode where the *mechanism* genuinely reproduces
at toy scale (an optimization runaway, not a representation phenomenon), so the calibration is not fighting its vehicle
the way P0.3 was. The two-sided gate passes on both poles, the leading-indicator payoff is real, the cross-mode
specificity check (grad norm stays bounded under collapse) is a genuine discriminator, and the two "vehicle" findings —
the loss must be *unbounded* (MAE MSE, P0.4.0) and, within MAE, *raw* (not `norm_pix`, P0.4.1) — are a non-obvious,
well-motivated pair. The distillates are already candid that the lead time is "most likely not generalizable." The code
behind all of this is trustworthy: the code-validity pass (E) found no result-changing bug, and it *confirms* two of the
findings below at the source (A3's truncation artifact, B1's name collision).

But the ✅ over-reads toward **operational readiness** in one load-bearing way the docs do *not* flag, and several
individual claims are stronger than the two-seed, single-operating-point data behind them. The single dominating gap:
**everything of value in this calibration is contingent on a training configuration the production system probably will
not use** — SGD + raw MSE. Under standard MAE pretraining (AdamW + `norm_pix`), the docs' own claims say the leading
indicator disappears and surprise detection disappears; what survives is a coincident NaN alarm that needs no
calibration (C1). That is the P0.4 analogue of the collapse audit's backbone mismatch (P0.2 C3) and the forgetting
audit's reader-availability gap (P0.3 C3).

Grouped below as over-claims (A), contradictions/tensions (B), structural gaps (C), conclusions the data supports but
the docs did not draw (D), and a code-validity check of the premise (E). **Where it should land after remediation:**
with A1/A2 promoted and C1 recorded, the ✅ restated as scoped to *SGD + raw-MSE MAE* — and, because Phase-1's
optimizer/loss is deliberately still open, P0.4 handing that choice a concrete design input: keep an undamped optimizer
with raw MSE to retain the leading-indicator and surprise reads, or accept grad norm as a coincident NaN alarm under
AdamW/`norm_pix`. That reframes the contingency from a silent readiness gap into the mode's defining, *actionable*
Phase-1 recommendation.

## Strengths (so the criticism is calibrated)

- **The two vehicle findings are the substudy's real contribution** and are correctly framed as "the vehicle changes
    what the instrument can see," not as nuisance. The nesting (unbounded loss → then raw-vs-normalized *within* MAE) is
    a clean, generalizable lesson.
- **Cross-mode specificity is a true discriminator, not a smoke test.** Showing the collapse PC rides its loss to the −1
    floor while its grad norm stays bounded (peak ~2.3) establishes the instrument fires on *divergence*, not "something
    bad in general" — the one thing that separates a useful instability detector from a generic alarm.
- **The distributional-OOD refutation (P0.4.1) is the headline and it is honest.** Reframing "surprise detector" down to
    "content-shock (error-magnitude) detector — *not* a distribution-shift detector" on the strength of the
    phase-scramble non-fire is exactly the kind of scope-narrowing most work omits.
- **The lead-time honesty is good.** Both the P0.4.0 Insights and the distillate state the lead is regime-specific and
    inversely coupled to severity (longest at threshold, shrinking toward a floor as instability worsens — see A4). This
    is a rare admission that the "payoff" is conditional.
- **The two-sided gate is genuinely two-sided here** (unlike the single-pass collapse corner, P0.2 B3): the healthy arm
    is a certified genuinely-learning MAE (final ≤ 0.9× initial, gated), not an under-developed relative reference.

## A. Over-claims / conclusions stronger than the evidence

**A1 — The load-bearing "AdamW damps the runaway to a no-lead knife-edge" claim has no preserved artifact.**

- **Issue** — This is the *key finding* of P0.4.0 (it promotes the vehicle from "MAE" to "MAE + SGD"), and the doc
    states specific numbers — "at `lr ∈ [1, 10]` the grad norm blows to `10³–10⁶` but the normalized step absorbs it …
    at `lr = 100` it goes to NaN in a single step." Yet every promoted artifact is SGD; there is no AdamW run in the
    tree. A conference reviewer would flag the paper's headline mechanistic claim as resting on an experiment described
    but not shown. §E confirms `optim=adamw` is a wired one-flag swap (`configs/optim/adamw.yaml` exists) — a
    seconds-long confirmatory run, not a research task.
- **Remediation** `[new runs]` — **highest-value, lowest-cost.** Run the P0.4.0 dose-response with `optim=adamw` and
    promote it as a small `P0.4.0/adamw_seed0` artifact (or a slim `{lr, peak_grad_norm, nonfinite_step, lead}` table);
    confirm the no-lead knife-edge. Until it exists, mark the AdamW numbers as "exploratory, not preserved" in the doc.
- **Conclusion** — **resolved — strengthens the ✅.** The AdamW dose-response was run and preserved
    (`artifacts/P0.4.0/adamw_seed0/`, seed 0): the grad norm blows to `21 → 4.8×10² → 5.4×10⁵` across `lr = 0.1, 1, 10`
    yet the normalized step **absorbs** every one (no non-finite step), and only at `lr = 100` does the loss go NaN — in
    a *single* step, measured lead `None`. The headline mechanistic claim is now shown, not merely described; the
    sane-LR AdamW reference (`lr = 10⁻³`) learns cleanly, so the no-lead knife-edge is the artifact-backed AdamW pole.
    Landed in [P0.4.0.md](../phase0/P0.4.0.md) (vehicle section + How-to-run) and its distillate. **What this buys us:**
    the AdamW pole is a cost, not an endorsement — it shows AdamW *erases* the leading indicator (the runaway is real
    but absorbed, so no lead), which argues *for* the SGD vehicle if early warning is wanted, not for AdamW. It is the
    first of the two poles C1 synthesises into the Phase-1 recommendation.

**A2 — The same gap applies to P0.4.1's `norm_pix` claim.**

- **Issue** — "A saturating noise shock under `norm_pix` gives only a ~1.5× grad blip" is the load-bearing justification
    for turning `norm_pix_loss` off — the second half of the nested vehicle finding — and it too has no artifact. §E
    confirms `ssl.norm_pix_loss=true` is a one-flag toggle over the existing gate config.
- **Remediation** `[new runs]` (seconds) + `[wording]` — Re-run the `burst_scale=4` gate with `norm_pix_loss=true` and
    record the ~1.5× blip as a promoted line (a natural companion to A1); or, if left unrun, label it "exploratory."
- **Conclusion** — **resolved — strengthens the ✅.** The `norm_pix_loss=true` gate was run and preserved
    (`artifacts/P0.4.1/norm_pix_seed0/`, seed 0): the base loss stays flat (`1.10 → 0.99`, no competence headroom) and
    the saturating `burst_scale=4` shock — which fires `78×` under raw MSE — gives a **1.47×** cross-arm grad blip,
    below the `2.0×` fire threshold, so the gate correctly does not fire (measured `1.47×` vs the doc's stated `~1.5×`).
    The second half of the nested vehicle finding is now artifact-backed. Landed in [P0.4.1.md](../phase0/P0.4.1.md)
    (vehicle section + How-to-run) and its distillate. **What this buys us:** likewise a cost, not an endorsement —
    `norm_pix` *erases* the surprise read (the shock falls below the fire threshold), which argues *for* the raw-MSE
    vehicle if change-point detection is wanted, not for `norm_pix`. It is the second pole C1 folds into the Phase-1
    recommendation.

**A3 — The headline grad-norm contrast (`1.2×10⁶×`) is a truncation artifact and is not portable; only the binary
separation is.**

- **Issue** — The "smallest diverging peak" that anchors the contrast is whichever arm NaNs *earliest* (fewest finite
    steps recorded before the blow-up), so it is not even monotone in LR: seed 0's min-diverging arm is `lr=10` (peak
    `5.2×10⁵`, above `lr=3`'s `6×10⁸`), seed 1's is `lr=30` (`1.3×10⁵`), batch-16's is `lr=3` (`1.5×10⁴`). The reported
    contrast consequently swings **`3.4×10⁴×` → `3.3×10⁵×` → `1.2×10⁶×`** across three runs of the *same* phenomenon — a
    ~35× spread. The verdict (every diverging arm clears the healthy peak by ≥ `min_separation`=100×; threshold `lr=3`;
    lead 5) is rock-solid; the specific `10⁶` number is not. This is the instability twin of the collapse audit's
    "magnitudes are thread/device-dependent, cite the verdict" (P0.2 A4). §E confirms the mechanism at the source: the
    gate computes `separation = min(firing-arm peaks) / healthy_peak`, and each arm's peak is the max over its *finite*
    steps only — so an arm that NaNs sooner records a lower peak, and the `min` picks it. Truncation-sensitive by
    construction; the ≥100× verdict is robust regardless.
- **Remediation** `[wording]` — Lead the distillate and `index.md` with the *verdict* (≥100× separation, threshold
    `lr=3`, 5-step lead, both seeds) and demote the `1.2×10⁶×` figure to an illustrative, non-portable example. No new
    runs.
- **Conclusion** — **clarified — no verdict change.** The load-bearing verdict (every diverging arm clears the healthy
    peak by `≥100×`; threshold `lr=3`; 5-step lead; both seeds) is unaffected; only the non-portable `1.2×10⁶×`
    magnitude was demoted to an explicitly truncation-sensitive illustration (a `min` over firing-arm peaks that swings
    ~35× across the seed/batch runs of the same phenomenon). The E1 test `test_gate_separation_is_min_over_firing_arms`
    now pins the property. Landed in [P0.4.0.md](../phase0/P0.4.0.md) Findings, its distillate, and the
    [index](../phase0/index.md) cell + artifacts note.

**A4 — "Lead time shrinks with severity" rests on n = 2 direct measurements.**

- **Issue** — The claim is stated crisply in both distillates, but the only *measured* lead times on the severity axis
    are the two runaway rungs of `scale_dose` (`scale 8 → 14`, `scale 16 → 5`); the transient rungs have no NaN, hence
    no lead, and P0.4.0's lead is a *single* point (the `lr=3` operating point, `lead=5`, identical across all three
    seeds/batches). The higher-LR arms (`lr=10, 30`) never had their spike-step logged, so P0.4.0 supports the coupling
    only through the *time-to-NaN* proxy (nonfinite step `8 → 5 → 4`), not through measured lead. So the direct evidence
    for the inverse lead↔severity law is two data points on one seed. The theoretical argument (geometric growth,
    per-step multiplier `|1 − LR·λ|`) is sound and makes it *plausible*, but "shrinks with severity" is presented as an
    established empirical trend on n = 2.
- **Remediation** `[wording]` + optional `[analysis]`/`[new runs]` — Soften to "consistent with the operating-envelope
    prediction (two runaway rungs: 14 → 5 steps)"; if a firmer trend is wanted, log the spike-step at `lr=10, 30` in
    P0.4.0 (post-hoc if the per-step traces survive, else a seconds-long re-run) to add points to the same curve.
- **Conclusion** — **resolved — strengthens the ✅.** The optional `[new runs]` were taken: a seeded data-axis
    densification of the runaway band (artifact `scale_dose_seeded/`, ~7 rungs × seeds 0/1/2) turns the n = 2 trend into
    an established, seed-stable inverse lead↔severity coupling (the artifact now seeds the *full* ladder 0/1/2 — the
    sub-threshold transient rungs reproduce too — with the median lead monotone in amplitude, per-seed spread ≤ 4 steps)
    — no longer "consistent with, not establishing." It also surfaces two findings the two points could not: the lead
    **floors at ~4 steps** (it does *not* shrink to zero, as the naive "→ 0 as severity worsens" reading assumed), and
    the transient→runaway threshold is a **seed-fragile knife-edge** where the lead is longest — so the
    operating-envelope now has a concrete *shape* (lead maximal at the edge, floored deep past it). The seed tables live
    in [P0.4.1.md](../phase0/P0.4.1.md) Findings + Insights and its distillate.

## B. Contradictions / internal tensions

**B1 — "Same word, two denominators": `change_ratio` (within-arm) vs the headline fire (cross-arm).**

- **Issue** — The design section defines the gate as "the shock's burst peak grad norm ≥ `change_ratio` × the control's
    same-step peak" — a *cross-arm* ratio. But the artifact's per-arm `change_ratio` field is a *within-arm* ratio
    (shock burst peak / that arm's own pre-burst base peak): at the operating point it reads **4.6×**, while the
    headline "78×" is the *cross-arm* fire (`fire_ratio` = shock burst peak / control burst peak). Both are legitimate,
    but they are different quantities sharing one name, and the doc's Reads bullet uses `change_ratio` for the cross-arm
    gate while the JSON uses it for the within-arm number. The user flagged this directly and it is a real source of
    confusion. §E confirms the collision is in the *code and config*, not only the prose: the harness computes a
    within-arm `change_ratio` summary field (`burst_peak / base_peak`) yet applies the config threshold
    `gate.change_ratio` to the *cross-arm* `fire_ratio`, and the config comment for `change_ratio` describes it as
    within-arm — so the config documents the old within-arm design while the code implements the cross-arm gate. The
    gate *math* is correct (cross-arm is the right quantity, matching the headline); only the naming is inconsistent.
- **Remediation** `[wording]` — Name them distinctly across doc, config comment, and the JSON field — e.g. `fire_ratio`
    (cross-arm, the gate) vs `within_arm_change_ratio` (the change-point sanity check) — and state which one the "78×"
    headline is (cross-arm).
- **Conclusion** — **resolved — strengthens the ✅.** The collision is gone across code, config, and doc: the cross-arm
    gate quantity is `fire_ratio` everywhere (config key `gate.fire_ratio`, the JSON output field, the prose) and the
    within-arm sanity-check quantity is `within_arm_ratio` (surfaced as `control_within_arm_ratio`); `change_ratio` no
    longer appears. Behaviour is unchanged — the gate math was already cross-arm-correct; only the naming was misaligned
    (the JSON output was in fact already renamed; the config key + comment + docstrings + prose were not). The E1 test
    `test_gate_keys_on_cross_arm_not_within_arm` pins that the gate keys on the cross-arm ratio (a both-arms-spike case
    is high within-arm yet ~1× cross-arm and must not fire). Landed in [P0.4.1.md](../phase0/P0.4.1.md) Reads,
    `scripts/positive_control_data_surprise.py`, and `configs/positive_control_data_surprise.yaml`.

**B2 — The "forcing lever is the data, not the learning rate" intro sits awkwardly with the `lr_sweep` negative
control.**

- **Issue** — P0.4.1's framing is that LR is held fixed at `lr_op` and data is the lever; the `lr_sweep` then re-runs
    the shock at `lr = 3, 10` to show LR-divergence is shock-independent. This is a legitimate negative control, but the
    doc introduces `lr_sweep` in the Reads/Findings without reconciling it against the "LR is constant" premise, which
    reads as a contradiction (the user flagged it). It is not one — `lr_op` is the operating point; `lr_sweep` is a
    deliberate off-operating-point control — but the text does not say so.
- **Remediation** `[wording]` — One clause: "LR is fixed at `lr_op` for the surprise gate; the `lr_sweep` is a separate
    *negative control* that deliberately raises LR to confirm warm-phase LR-divergence is shock-independent."
- **Conclusion** — **clarified — no verdict change.** One clause added: the gate is read with LR fixed at `lr_op` (data
    is the sole lever), and the `lr_sweep` is a separate off-operating-point negative control — not a contradiction of
    the fixed-LR premise but a control against it. Landed in [P0.4.1.md](../phase0/P0.4.1.md) Reads.

**B3 — Measurement-window ambiguity: the fire is read *during* the burst, recovery *after* it — the doc does not say so
plainly.**

- **Issue** — The user asked at what point the reading is taken. From the artifacts it is unambiguous — `fire_ratio` /
    `surprise_ratio` are computed over the burst window (steps 300–308), `recover_ratio` / `tail_min_grad_norm` over the
    tail (post-308) — but the prose blends "spikes then recovers" without stating that the *fire* verdict is a
    burst-window measurement and the *transient-vs-runaway* verdict is a tail measurement. §E confirms the windowing in
    `_summarize`: base `[init_skip, warm)`, burst `[warm, warm+burst)`, tail `[warm+burst, end)`, with fire/surprise
    read on the burst window and recovery on the tail — exactly as claimed.
- **Remediation** `[wording]` — State the two windows explicitly (fire = burst window; recovery = tail), and that
    Finding #1's fire is a `burst_kind=noise` (saturating) shock while the refutation is `burst_kind=dataset` (the other
    user question).
- **Conclusion** — **clarified — no verdict change.** The two windows are now stated plainly: fire (surprise +
    change-point) is read over the burst window `[300, 308)`, recovery (transient vs. runaway) over the tail; and
    Finding #1's fire is a `burst_kind=noise` (saturating) shock while the refutation is `burst_kind=dataset`. The E1
    test `test_summarize_windows_and_within_arm_ratio` pins the window reduction. Landed in
    [P0.4.1.md](../phase0/P0.4.1.md) Design.

## C. Structural gaps to close before Phase 1

**C1 — [Highest] The whole valuable calibration is contingent on one training config (SGD + raw MSE); the docs never
characterize it under the other candidate config (AdamW + `norm_pix`) Phase-1 might choose.**

- **Issue** — Standard MAE/ViT pretraining uses **AdamW** and **`norm_pix_loss=true`**. By the substudies' *own* claims,
    under that config: (i) the grad-norm **lead time vanishes** (AdamW damps the runaway to a one-step knife-edge, A1),
    and (ii) **data-surprise becomes invisible** (`norm_pix` caps the per-patch loss ~1, A2). What is left is grad norm
    as a *coincident* NaN detector — trivial, needing no calibration. So the two things that make P0.4 worth a substudy
    — the **leading indicator** and the **change-point / surprise** read — are precisely the two that config is claimed
    to erase. Phase-1's optimizer/loss is *not* yet committed — Phase 0 is meant to inform that choice — so this is not
    a gap against a fixed production target but an incomplete map of a design space Phase-1 will pick from: the ✅ is
    earned for "SGD + raw-MSE MAE" and silent on whether *any* early-warning value ports to the AdamW + `norm_pix` pole.
    It is nonetheless the exact shape of P0.2 C3 (collapse suite is joint-embedding/terminal-BN-specific) and P0.3 C3
    (readers presuppose retained task-A data): a calibration whose value is *conditional* on a vehicle choice.
- **Remediation** `[wording]` + `[defer → Phase-1]`, fed by A1/A2 — Since Phase-1's config is deliberately open, record
    the contingency in `index.md` as a *design input to Phase-1*, not a decision owed now: if the loop wants the
    grad-norm early-warning value (leading indicator + surprise change-point), it should favor an undamped optimizer +
    raw MSE; if it adopts AdamW + `norm_pix`, grad norm degrades to a *coincident* NaN detector and an early-warning
    instability signal under adaptive optimizers becomes a Phase-1 open problem (no instrument here provides it). A1/A2
    supply the two poles of that map. Flag it as the mode's *defining* scope caveat, not folded silently into the ✅.
- **Conclusion** — **resolved — strengthens the ✅.** With A1/A2 preserved, both poles of the contingency are now mapped,
    so C1 converts from a silent readiness gap into an actionable **design recommendation** recorded in the
    [index](../phase0/index.md) status cell (and both distillates): to keep the grad-norm early-warning value (leading
    indicator + surprise change-point), favour an undamped optimizer + raw MSE; under AdamW + `norm_pix` grad norm
    degrades to a coincident NaN alarm and an early-warning instability signal is a Phase-1 open problem (no instrument
    here provides it). Because Phase-1's optimizer/loss is deliberately open, no config *decision* is owed — P0.4's job
    is only to inform that choice, which it now does. The critical path is unblocked.

**C2 — The severity/lead-time and batch-robustness reads are single-seed; only the two-sided gate is replicated.**

- **Issue** — The core gate is 2 seeds (good). But the batch-size ablation (`batch16_seed0`), the amplitude
    dose-response (`scale_dose`, seed 0), and the cross-mode specificity read (off the collapse harness, unpreserved)
    are each **n = 1**. The batch claim ("not a batch-size artifact") rests on a single alternative batch size (128 →
    16\) at one seed; the severity ladder and its lead-time trend (A4) are one seed. Cheaper to shore up here than
    anywhere else in Phase 0 (seconds/run), so the single-seed status is a choice worth revisiting rather than an
    inherent limit.
- **Remediation** `[new runs]` (low priority, very cheap) + `[wording]` — Either add a seed to the `scale_dose` and
    `batch16` reads, or explicitly label them "single-seed, illustrative" (the gate itself is 2-seed). Promote the
    cross-mode specificity grad-norm trace as a tiny artifact rather than "reads off the collapse harness."
- **Conclusion** — **resolved — strengthens the ✅.** All three single-seed reads were multi-seeded (the cheap
    replication was taken over labelling): the `scale_dose` amplitude ladder to seeds 0/1/2 (via A4,
    `scale_dose_seeded/`), and the `batch16` ablation + the cross-mode specificity trace to seeds 0/1/2 on HPU (new
    artifacts `batch16_seeded/` / `collapse_specificity/`). All three reproduce the verdict seed-robustly: batch16 gives
    an *identical* threshold (`lr=3`) and 5-step lead on every seed (healthy holds, gate passes), and the collapsing PC
    keeps its grad norm bounded (peak `~1.9–2.5`, never non-finite) on every seed — grad norm does not fire on collapse.
    The only seed-varying quantity is the (non-portable, A3) separation magnitude. This also promotes the cross-mode
    specificity trace the remediation asked for — no longer "a single trace off the collapse harness." Landed in
    [P0.4.0.md](../phase0/P0.4.0.md) / [P0.4.1.md](../phase0/P0.4.1.md) Findings.

**C3 — Specificity is tested against collapse only, not against forgetting or a healthy shift.**

- **Issue** — Cross-mode specificity established grad norm does not fire on *collapse*. It is untested against the
    *forgetting* PC (P0.3) and — more relevant — against a benign in-regime change (the P0.4.1 distributional-OOD
    non-fire partly covers this: grad norm correctly ignores a structure-destroying but in-range shift, a genuine
    benign-shift null). Forgetting is a slow representation phenomenon and almost certainly will not spike the grad
    norm, but "specific to divergence" is asserted on one of the two sibling modes.
- **Remediation** `[analysis]` or `[defer → Phase-1]` — Note that specificity is established vs collapse + vs in-range
    distribution shift (P0.4.1), and that vs forgetting it is expected-but-unchecked; a one-line read off any P0.3 MAE
    run's grad-norm trace would close it cheaply.
- **Conclusion** — **resolved — strengthens the ✅.** The forgetting leg is now *shown*, not predicted — specificity
    holds against all three sibling nulls: collapse (grad norm bounded `~1.9–2.5` on the P0.2 PC), a benign in-range
    shift (the P0.4.1 distributional-OOD non-fire), and now *forgetting* (artifact `forgetting_specificity/`, seeds
    0/1/2). On the P0.3.9 seed-stable SSL-MAE crater — every seed genuinely forgets (PC BWT `−0.065…−0.075`) — the
    phase-B grad norm peaks at only `~4–8` and stays finite, ~4 orders below the divergence scale and indistinguishable
    from the healthy arm, because forgetting is a slow representation drift, not an optimization instability. The
    remediation was heavier than the anticipated "one-line read": the P0.3 harness pre-dates the P0.4.0 grad-norm
    instrumentation, so no trace existed — the forgetting harness's `_train` was given an additive pre-clip grad-norm
    capture (default off, so no behaviour change) and re-run on HPU. Landed in [P0.4.0.md](../phase0/P0.4.0.md)
    Findings.

## D. Conclusions the data supports but the docs missed

**D1 — The healthy operating LR sits close to the divergence threshold — a narrow safe band worth stating.**

- **Issue** — The healthy reference is `lr=1` and the threshold is `lr=3`: a **3× margin**. Moreover `lr=1` is *not* the
    best-learning arm — `lr=0.1` descends further (seed 0: final 0.66× init vs `lr=1`'s 0.74×), and `lr=1` clears the
    "learns" bar (≤ 0.9×) only modestly. So without warmup/clip the SGD-MAE has a thin corridor between "learns best"
    and "diverges" (~sub-decade in LR). This is a positive framing of *why* the leading indicator matters (small
    overshoots are easy) and a concrete toy-scale operating-envelope datum.
- **Remediation** `[wording]` — State the ~3× healthy→threshold margin and that the chosen healthy LR is a
    deliberately-near-threshold reference (warmup/clip off), not the best learner. No new runs.
- **Conclusion** — **new supporting finding.** The docs now state the narrow safe band: healthy `lr=1` vs threshold
    `lr=3` is a 3× margin, and `lr=1` is a deliberately near-threshold reference, not the best learner (`lr=0.1`
    descends to `0.66×` init vs `lr=1`'s `0.74×`, verified against `dose_seed0`) — a concrete toy-scale
    operating-envelope datum and a positive framing of why the leading indicator matters (a small LR overshoot is easy
    to hit). Landed in [P0.4.0.md](../phase0/P0.4.0.md) Findings.
